Biography & Philosophy

Observing, not directing.

+ +

I began photographing fifteen years ago with a mechanical 35mm camera gifted by my grandfather. That grounding taught me to measure every frame before releasing the shutter: waiting for light to fall across a room, noticing how hands rest when people are at ease, and never fabricating an emotion that did not happen on its own.

Today my practice is split between documentary commissions of weddings across India, private portrait sessions, and ongoing photographic surveys of architecture and natural geography.

In my wedding commissions, I refuse to turn a couple's private celebration into a choreographed film set. I do not bring artificial smoke machines, strobe banks that blind elderly relatives, or clipboards with ninety staged Pinterest poses. My presence is gentle, observant, and quiet. The images that remain thirty years from now are the honest ones: your grandmother whispering into your ear, your uncle laughing with a drink in hand, and the exhaustion and grace of the final hour.
